About Me

I am a Computer Engineering student at the University of Massachusetts Amherst with a focus on embedded systems, firmware development, and hardware–software integration. I enjoy building robust end-to-end solutions, from writing low-level drivers to designing custom PCBs and 3D printed enclosures.

Through my personal projects, I have honed my technical expertise and problem-solving abilities. My largest is Plant-Buddy, a BLE soil-sensor probe and Raspberry Pi hub that I built end to end: a 4-layer ESP32-C3 board designed in KiCad, ESP-IDF firmware with a ~5.4 µA sleep cycle, and a Python hub with a FastAPI API and PWA dashboard. Earlier projects include developing firmware and designing a custom PCB for a DIY camera using a Teensy 4.1, ArduCam Mega, and an LED display, writing drivers for the BNO055 IMU on a Raspberry Pi Pico, and creating a high-performance terminal text editor in C. I have also built a linear regression model to predict house prices and explored real-time visual processing in C++.

Beyond the classroom, I've gained practical experience through student engineering organizations, collaborating with peers to deliver reliable results. Recently, I have been working with my design group on the UMass CubeSat Team to develop a custom battery management unit (BMU) and power distribution unit (PDU). This work has included writing a research paper outlining a comprehensive radiation protection plan for the power system in a 3–5 year sun-synchronous orbit and co-designing the PDU, a 4-layer, 225-part board released for fabrication in April 2026. I also designed a 4-layer development board for the team's Main Computer, built around a commercial stand-in for the radiation-tolerant SAMD21RT.

I enjoy exploring new advancements in technology, experimenting with innovative ideas, and learning about emerging trends in computer engineering. I'm looking for full-time roles in embedded systems, low-level software, and hardware design after I graduate in May 2027, where I can continue developing my skills and building reliable, real-world systems.
